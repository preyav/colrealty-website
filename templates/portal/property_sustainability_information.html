General Field Help:

Sustainable elements used in construction, without naming specific elements with ratings that may change over time.

  • Conserving Methods: Construction is planned to use fewer materials while maintaining structural integrity. This may include advanced wall framing or indigenous methods such as straw bale, sod or clay, based on local climate, materials and practices.
  • Onsite Recycling Center: The property includes built-in storage or containers for temporary storage of recyclable materials or compost collection.
  • Recyclable Materials: The structure includes multiple products with significant postconsumer recycled content. Major green building programs often use 25 percent postconsumer recycled content as a threshold. Postindustrial recycled content may also count, but to a lesser extent. Examples include masonry, paving stones, foundations with fly ash, aluminum gutters and downspouts, decking, drywall fibers, insulation, house wrap, vinyl plastics, countertops, cabinets, interior doors and trim.
  • Recycled Materials: The structure includes multiple products with significant postconsumer recycled content. Major green building programs often use 25 percent postconsumer recycled content as a threshold. Postindustrial recycled content may also count, but to a lesser extent. Examples include masonry, paving stones, foundations with fly ash, aluminum gutters and downspouts, decking, drywall fibers, insulation, house wrap, vinyl plastics, countertops, cabinets, interior doors and trim.
  • Regionally-Sourced Materials: Building materials were manufactured, extracted, harvested or recovered within 500 miles of the building. Several major green building programs use a 500-mile radius.
  • Renewable Materials: Naturally occurring, abundant or fast-growing materials. Examples include flooring and wood products from renewable sources, and bio-based materials processed from paper, straw, soy, natural fibers or crops.
  • Salvaged Materials: Materials diverted from landfill or sourced to give an otherwise used item a fresh use as an attached fixture. Examples include bricks, timbers, roofing, flooring, walls, cabinets and doors.
